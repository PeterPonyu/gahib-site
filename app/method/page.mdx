# Method

GAHIB fits a variational autoencoder on single-cell RNA-seq counts. The
encoder can be a multilayer perceptron, a Transformer, or a graph neural
network (GAT, GCN, GraphSAGE, ChebConv, TAG, GraphTransformer, ARMA), and
the latent representation is shaped by three losses that together produce a
hierarchy-aware embedding.

## Three losses

**Reconstruction.** A count likelihood — negative-binomial, zero-inflated
negative-binomial, Poisson, or zero-inflated Poisson — fits the raw counts.

**Information bottleneck.** A second decoder reconstructs the input from a
2-D manifold coordinate, compressing the latent into a low-dimensional
representation suitable for visualization and trajectory analysis.

**Lorentz hyperbolic loss.** Anchors the manifold coordinate on the
hyperboloid model of hyperbolic space. The Lorentz distance between two
points on the hyperboloid is

$$
d_\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \operatorname{arcosh}(-\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}\rangle_\mathcal{L})
$$

where the Minkowski inner product is

$$
\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}\rangle_\mathcal{L} = -u_0 v_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{d} u_i v_i.
$$

Radial distance from the origin then encodes hierarchy: cells closer to
the origin sit higher in the developmental tree, and lineage divergence
maps to angular separation.

## Important constraint

The hyperbolic loss is degenerate when the bottleneck coordinate is
untrained (i.e., when the bottleneck reconstruction weight is zero). The
implementation enforces this dependency.

## Optional trajectory modules

Stochastic differential equation (SDE) and partial differential equation
(PDE) modules are provided for trajectory experiments. These are off by
default and are documented in the source.
